# Colour from Theme Vocabulary

**Status: ACTIVE.** Epic: `candybar-render-ai7`. Evidence: `design-docs/colour-demo.html`.

Tracking tickets: the epic `candybar-render-ai7` and its children, and the epic `candybar-theme-colours-6fq5`, which put each theme's own shades on the closed bar. The tickets are deliberately short and link here; this document is the only home for the reasoning.

**Supersedes the hueStep thread.** `STATUSLINE-THEMING.md` is itself marked SUPERSEDED — its three-layer model was absorbed into `PROPOSAL-segment-dsl.md` — but it is where per-segment hue rotation was first proposed, as `brandon-segment-dsl-segment-render-wd5.4` ("`hueStep` threading through palette resolution"). That thread shipped as `hue.step` and is what this document ends. The rest of `STATUSLINE-THEMING.md` stays superseded by the DSL proposal, unchanged.

## The rule

> Row n of the bar starts on step n of the variation — an ordered list of the theme's decorative roles, `secondary` then `accent` by default (the `alt` variation also steps along a row, every three cells) — and each cell of the row wears one of that role's own shades, from the window the shade dial is turned to: three neighbouring rungs of the ladder Textual generates for every role, from `darken-3` through the role itself to `lighten-3`, centred on the role by default; or, at the dial's lowest position, the role's muted form. The row is the innermost of the rows the cell's address stacks through; the shade is the cell's place on that row's line, counted over the row's leaves through any `{ h }` or `{ v }` nested in it, because those cells sit side by side with the row's own. Edit mode's chrome alone shares the place of the cell it edits. Every colour a closed cell wears is one its palette names. An open trigger wears its menu's hue at the shade the dial names for the pressed state (`accent-muted` on the bar at every window of the ladder), and its menu's rows sit on `surface`.

The roles are Textual's, and the themes are Textual themes: *primary* is the branding colour, *secondary* an alternative "to differentiate from primary", *accent* is "used sparingly to draw attention", *surface* the default widget background, and a role's *muted* form is the role blended most of the way into the background, which Textual's labels and flat buttons wear under the role's own text colour. (Until brandon-theme-picker-bgw.8fp this was one sRGB `mix(base, hue, amount)` over all three hues, chosen per cell, and until candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.e5n each cell wore one of three tones blended between `surface`, `background` and its row's hue; see Decisions.)

It **selects** from the theme; it never **synthesizes** a new colour. Until e5n the tones were blends no palette contains, and until candybar-joiners-e5r4.dg6 an open trigger and its bands were searched to stand apart from their neighbours; now every background decoration chooses, closed or open, is its palette's own entry, byte for byte (`test/decor.test.ts`). The rest of this document says why, and what had to be measured first; `design-docs/PROPOSAL-joiners.md` says why the open menu's search went.

## What `hue.step` did

`hue.step` was a global pre-order counter over segment leaves. Leaf *i* gets `hueShift = i * 14` degrees, applied to the whole theme palette through `transposePalette`. A segment's colour is therefore a function of **how many leaves precede it anywhere in the tree** — not of what it is, not of where it sits, not of what it belongs to.

Solving the live bar's emitted RGB back to hue indices shows what that buys. Visible segments sit at indices 2, 5, 8, 11 (row 1) and 22, 25, 28, 31, 34, 37 (row 2). Nothing visible is at index 0. Adjacent visible segments are exactly 3 apart, because gated-off edit-mode chrome (the `+`/`-` affordances) is interleaved between every pair and the cursor advances over hidden leaves by design. Verification deltas were 1–7 out of 255, which is screenshot sampling noise.

Roughly two-thirds of the bar's hue budget is spent on segments that never render. `model` is teal because 22 invisible things precede it.

It also laps. At step 14 the hue circle closes every ~25.7 leaves; the bar has ~38. Segment 5 and segment 31 receive the same hue, so "distance along the walk" stops meaning "difference in colour" a third of the way in.

## Why hue rotation was abandoned entirely

Two measured facts killed it, and neither is fixable by tuning the step.

**Rotation cannot manufacture saturation a role lacks.** atom-one-dark `surface` is rgb(59,65,77). Every rotation of a near-grey is another near-grey: hue-only across 7 segments moved #3B414D to #4A3E34 and stayed grey throughout. Adding chroma scaling gets colour (×2.5 gives #325E5E..#5A3A21; ×5 more saturated still) but that is no longer rotation, it is inventing a palette the theme does not have.

**Spanning the full hue circle destroys theme identity, because theme identity lives in hue.** With enough rotation to be visible, every theme collapsed to the same bar at a different lightness. dracula stopped looking like dracula. This was the decisive failure: the mechanism's own success condition is indistinguishable from erasing the thing it is decorating.

Narrowing the span does not rescue it, and costs on both axes. Measuring minimum adjacent separation against minimum separation between *any* pair, over 10 cells:

| placement | min adjacent | min any pair |
|---|---|---|
| today (14°/leaf, 42° between visible neighbours, unbounded) | 42 | 18 (it laps) |
| bounded 85° span | 21 | 5.3 |
| van der Corput, full circle | 68 | 22.5 |
| true subdivision, full circle, rows on disjoint arcs | 45 | 22.5 |

Bounding the span is worse on *both* measures than the thing it was meant to improve. And subdivision degrades more gracefully than stepping as the bar grows. Minimum separation between *any* pair, subdivision vs today: 7 cells 45.0 vs 42.0, 10 cells 22.5 vs 18.0, 20 cells 11.3 vs 6.0, 26 cells 11.3 vs 6.0. Bit reversal's closest pair is 360°/2^⌈log₂ n⌉ apart, so it halves only when the count crosses a power of two; stepping's closest pair is whichever 42°·d, for d < n, lands nearest a full turn, which is 6° as soon as d = 17 is in range.

The general lesson, which the rest of the design is built on: cohesion has to come from the axes that do **not** vary — lightness and chroma held constant — not from narrowing the axis that carries the variety. That is ai7's lesson; since e5n the closed bar holds neither constant: a cell wears its role's own shade, at whatever lightness and chroma the theme gives it (see "The theme already has a vocabulary").

## The theme already has a vocabulary

A Textual theme carries 151 entries: about 11 root hues, each with `darken-1..3` / `lighten-1..3` ramps, plus `-background` and `-muted` variants and `text-` colours for the roles. Three roots are non-semantic and therefore free for decoration: `primary`, `secondary`, `accent`.

Tinting the theme's own neutral surface toward its own hues produces per-theme sets that are unmistakably that theme's:

| theme | three decorative tints |
|---|---|
| dracula | `#45405d` purple, `#353a4e` blue, `#513c54` pink |
| gruvbox | `#494c48` sage, `#4f4a44` tan, `#5e5035` gold |
| nord | `#495969` ice, `#485366` steel, `#515062` mauve |
| catppuccin-latte | `#d5c9ef` lavender, `#e4d8da` warm, `#ead1c6` peach |

This needs no light/dark branch. The base carries the lightness, so a light theme tints correctly under the identical expression — catppuccin-latte's row above comes out of the same `mix` as dracula's.

Picking raw palette entries instead of tinting does not work. `primary-background` is `#5a566c` on dracula — a purple surface, exactly right — and `#8839ef` on catppuccin-latte, a saturated purple at luminance 87 on a theme whose surface sits at luminance 233. The entry names a role, not a lightness, so only the mix can hold the lightness constant across themes.

**Answered by Brandon's pick** (candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.e5n). The measurement still holds — an entry names a role, not a lightness — but holding the lightness constant across themes is no longer the aim. Shown every bundled theme's bar in its roles' own shades beside the tints, Brandon chose the shades, so the bar wears a role's own shades by design, at whatever lightness the theme gives them. Text is chosen for whatever background results, at the same 4.5:1 floor as every other cell, so legibility does not depend on where that lightness lands. And the shade dial is how a user moves the lightness: its window slides from a role's three darkest shades to its three brightest, and below the darkest it reaches the role's muted form.

## The region model

Confusion got solved three separate times by the same move: give each concern its **own region** rather than nudging things apart inside one shared region.

| region | occupant | boundary |
|---|---|---|
| tint (the row's role, at the dial's shades) | ordinary bar segments | a shade the palette names for the row's role: three neighbouring rungs of its ladder, or its muted form; never a blend |
| state | an open disclosure's trigger, and the ✕ and blank leads of its band's rows | the lineage's hue at its depth (`accent` on the bar), at the shade the dial names for the pressed state: the role's muted form, or `lighten-2` where the row is already muted |
| plane | the backing under a band's rows, at every depth | `surface` |
| items | the cells on a band | the band's hue at the tones of the dial's window |
| absent | error / success / warning | not in the decorative vocabulary at all |

The last row is the strongest guarantee in the design. Decoration cannot collide with meaning because meaning is **not in the set**. That is strictly stronger than the hue-anchoring it replaces, which only stopped semantic colours from moving while leaving decorative colours free to arrive at them.

Since e5n the guarantee is about roles, not colours. A closed cell wears its role's own shades, so a theme that spells a decorative role in an alert's colour puts that colour on calm cells. Among the bundled themes, four decorative shades sit within ΔE .02 of an alert (measured over every theme's palette): textual-dark's and textual-light's `accent` is their `warning`, `#FEA62B`; monokai's `secondary` is its `error`, `#F82672`; and catppuccin-mocha's `accent-lighten-2` sits .018 from its `warning`, on the dial's two brightest positions.

The state boundary had to be enforced rather than assumed, because "the pure hue is vivid" is false for some themes. textual-dark's `secondary` is `#004578`, a navy already darker than most surfaces; its pure form sat on top of the decorative tints at contrast 1.42, indistinguishable. textual-ansi's `primary` was worse, at 1.14. The first enforcement (ai7.2) started the state colour at the rule's own form near the top of its range, `mix(surface, hue, 0.92)`, and pushed it toward `foreground` — as far as `foreground` itself — until contrast against the most-tinted cell of that same hue, on every base, reached 2.2. Measured global minimum across 23 themes × 3 hues × 3 bases afterwards: 2.21, up from 1.14; solarized-dark's `secondary` was the one that needed the full push. Themes that were already vivid stopped at step zero and were byte-unchanged, and a hue that could not clear the floor even at `foreground` was a failure the demo threw on. Since e5n the search started from the hue exactly as the palette names it and slid it along its own lightness, held off the closed cells of its own row; the e5n entry in Decisions says why. Since dg6 nothing is enforced between cells: the state is a named shade, and where a theme names two neighbours alike the joiner shows the seam (the dg6 entry in Decisions).

### Three disclosure rules

**A band is a plane.** Its plane is the theme's `surface`, at every depth: a plane is the backing behind a band's rows, and a nested band is told by its leads and its hue, not by its backing (dg6; see Decisions). Its items are the band's hue at the tones of the dial's window, as the palette names them, so a menu is a variation on the colour of the header that opened it (brandon-menus-sjhy.4fi).

**Depth steps along the lineage.** Every bar trigger opens in the first hue of its variation's `Lineage` (`Variation.open`; the shipped rows: `accent`, then `primary`, then `secondary`, so a nested disclosure takes the next, and a one-hue lineage keeps its hue however deep it nests). The hue is the one cue depth carries: the plane is `surface` at every depth, and the bundled 🍫 → tab → ⚙ → style list reaches depth 3. Until dg6 each depth's plane also receded one step further from its trigger, held a ΔE floor off the plane it hung in; the dg6 entry in Decisions says why that went.

**A trigger is drawn from what it opens, not from where it sits.** It takes the peak of its children's region. Without this, a submenu's parent is coloured as one of its siblings and nothing connects it to the thing it opened. In code a node's position is a `Region` (`src/themes/decor.ts`): on the bar, an address into the vocabulary; on a band, the `Disclosure` its trigger was dealt plus the steps since the body's root — so a body needs nothing of its trigger's position, only what the trigger opened, which is why the body hangs on the trigger node rather than beside it.

### One distribution field, at every level

Placement inside a region is a single per-instance field: the instance's **distribution**. A bar row is an instance and a menu band is an instance, and both use that one field — a menu is itself an instance, so its band's placement is just that instance's distribution. The demo exposes it as two dropdowns — `distribution` for the bar, `menu distribution` for the bands — over one table: both levels read the same `f(i, n)`, the band reading it into its items as the bar reads a cell into its shades (brandon-menus-sjhy.b9w: scaled into a plane-to-state window, neighbouring cells landed under the ΔE .04 at which the joiner draws a divider instead of an arrow). There is one mechanism here, not two.

Five distributions ship — van der Corput (the default), golden angle, monotonic, ends-interleaved, and `uniform`, the constant where every item is identical. This is one type with five instances, not five code paths: the distribution is a value the instance carries, so adding a fifth is data, not structure.

Measured across 6 items, a monotonic ramp spreads lightness by 40 (dracula) / 50 (latte), and reads as a directional wedge — an ordering the items may or may not have. That was measured over the old 18-entry vocabulary; on the bar a row's cells now choose among the three shades of the dial's window, read round a circle in the order the window lists them (low, high, middle; see the e5n entry in Decisions), so a monotonic row of four lands low, high, middle, low, and from five cells on some neighbours share a shade.

**Isolation is a property of the chosen distribution, not of the system.** `vdc(i)` is bit reversal (0, .5, .25, .75, .125, …) and never reads `n`, the sibling count; golden angle doesn't either. Under those two, adding, removing or hiding a sibling moves nobody — verified by adding 25 menu entries and watching zero bar cells change. `monotonic` = `(i+0.5)/n` and `ends-interleaved` both read `n`, so choosing either forfeits isolation for that instance: one new sibling re-spaces every existing one in it.

Neither half is wrong, and the author picks knowingly. A menu's option domain is a *closed* set — the count is the set itself, not an accident of authoring — so an ordered distribution is legitimate there, and its directional ramp may be exactly what the ordering deserves. An open, hand-authored bar row is where isolation matters, and it is why van der Corput is the default: it is `n`-free, and unlike golden angle its recursive bisection mirrors the tree it orders. Isolation is also precisely what `hue.step` never had — under it a hidden leaf anywhere upstream re-colours everything after it, whatever the author intended.

Directional cues need no light/dark branch either. Mixing toward `foreground` (active) and toward `background` (recessed plane) inverts automatically: measured open-vs-closed is +43 on dracula, +37 on nord, −41 on catppuccin-latte, −47 on textual-light; band-vs-open is −56, −56, +60, +66. Those are ai7's mechanism and numbers. Since dg6 the open state is its hue's muted form, blended most of the way into the theme's own background, so it recedes on a dark theme and pales on a light one — still with no branch on the theme's polarity.

## Rejected alternatives

Each of these was considered and measured. They are recorded with their reasons so they are not re-proposed.

**Per-segment authored `bg:` colour math** (`{{ shiftHue (color "surface") 14 }}`). This is what CLAUDE.md currently recommends as the replacement direction, and it is wrong: it is hand-curation of every segment, which is explicitly ruled out.

**Bounded hue span (85°) for cohesion.** Measured 21 / 5.3 against the full circle's 45 / 22.5. Bounding costs both local contrast and global uniqueness.

**Full-circle hue rotation.** Destroys theme identity, because theme identity lives in hue.

**Emphasis by degree for the open state** (make it *more* tinted). Fails for the same reason hue rotation did: the decorative vocabulary already spans a wide band of tints, so "more tinted" lands inside the space other cells occupy. Hence disjoint regions rather than a stronger nudge.

**Making rich-js `ThemeKey` a vector space / Lie group so hue offsets compose.** Moot — there are no hue offsets any more. Do not build it. The design needs `getThemePalette`, `p.get(role)`, a colour mix, and a contrast ratio, all of which rich-js already has.

**A `theme.dark` branch for directional cues.** Unnecessary: mixing toward `foreground` / `background` inverts on its own.

## Decisions

**Scope is backgrounds only.** `fg:` specs and `GIT_QUIET_FG` are authorial de-emphasis, not decoration, and stay as they are. One exception: text on a state cell is contrast-chosen, because a fixed foreground measurably fails on pure hues — AA on dark themes, AA-large on light, with solarized-light the floor at 3.13:1.

**Text nobody authored is chosen on every cell, not only state cells** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.b2g; this supersedes the text half of the decision above — state cells included, so solarized-light's 3.13:1 is gone). Bar cells used to keep the terminal's own text, on the premise that it reads on whatever the theme paints behind it. A contrast audit of every bundled theme under a dark and a light terminal measured that premise false in every theme for one polarity or the other: 1.05:1 for the dark themes under a light terminal, and the same for the light themes under a dark one. The bar paints its own background, so its text must come from the same place. An unauthored `fg:` is now `textOn` of the background the cell resolves to, in every region. `textOn` floors its pick at `TEXT_MIN_CONTRAST`, WCAG AA's 4.5:1 for normal text, because on a mid-luminance tint neither pole clears (atom-one-dark's foreground measured 2.49:1). The pole taken is the one on the side rich-js `contrastFor` names, and a slide deepens it that way: the pole that merely measures better can sit on the far side of the background (39 of the registry's 828 tints and band colours), and sliding it would carry its hue through the background into the other polarity. 3:1 was tried first; it passed the audit but looked washed out on the saturated threshold colours, and a statusline is small text. Authored semantic text in the bundled segments (git accents, the cache timer) goes through `readableOn` at the same floor, which moves lightness only and so keeps the hue that carries the meaning. Quiet git structure keeps its own 3:1: it is meant to recede. `pnpm gallery` renders every theme with one payload for looking at this again. (Since candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.e5n a cell at the dial's `muted` position is written in its role's own `text-<role>`, floored the same way.)

**A tint carries its theme's chroma, not its surface's** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.8fp). Superseded by the e5n entry below, except for the calm arm: a closed cell is now a shade its palette names, so no computed tint carries a chroma share, and the state search no longer passes through `foreground`. One `mix(base, hue, amount)` with `amount ≤ 0.30` made every tint 70–84% surface. Measured with `pnpm gallery` and ΔE_OK: the bar wore 19–57% of each theme's accent chroma, and two distinct themes' bars sat as close as ΔE .018 cell by cell (dracula and rose-pine-moon), under what the eye resolves. Raising the amount moved lightness toward the hue too, onto the state region, and broke 13 of the floors below. Three changes fixed it, and every floor in `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts` held unchanged on the bundled themes. Under a look they did not all hold: `dim` pulls atom-one-dark's foreground itself to 2.19 against its primary tints, so the state search, which stopped at `foreground`, threw (three solarized-dark look combinations already did before this change). The search now continues past `foreground` along its own lightness (rich-js `ensureContrast`, folded over the hue's tints), and the floor is pinned over every theme × bundled look:

- **Chroma is decoupled from lightness.** A tint is `base.mixAxes(hue, { l: amount, c: 0.8, h: 1, alpha: 0 })` (rich-js `Oklch.mixAxes`). Every floor here is a contrast ratio, and lightness decides contrast, so the floors see what they saw before while the tint carries 0.8 of the hue's chroma, or all the sRGB gamut holds at that lightness on pale light-theme surfaces. The minimum cell-wise distance between two themes rose from .018 to .029, between rose-pine and rose-pine-moon, two variants of one family.
- **Hue was made the vocabulary's fastest axis** so neighbours would differ in hue. It measured well and looked wrong; the next decision replaces it.
- **A threshold's calm arm is the tint.** `{{ tint }}` returns the decoration the segment's region dealt it, available in `bg:` itself, and the bundled cascades end their calm arm on `(tint)`. A calm cell states nothing, so it wears decoration. The fixed `panel` it replaces painted the calm block and weekly as one slab.

**A row is one hue; its cells differ by tone** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.8fp, second pass). Hue at every cell alternated two saturated hues seam by seam, and Brandon rejected it on sight: "you end up with this 'sports team' sort of look … like im either a college or an NFL team." Decoration now follows the Textual roles the themes were written with:

- **The bar hues are `primary` and `secondary`, one per row.** A row is the innermost of the rows an address stacks through before it reaches a cell, so a row stacked above the whole bar (edit mode's reset banner) recolours no row beneath it, a `{ v }` nested inside a row stacks no bar rows (its children are places on the row's lines), and a bar with no vertical container is one row, in the first hue. The row's hue is the half its placement falls in: van der Corput's first bit, so rows alternate however many the bar stacks. They alternate among siblings, not rendered lines: a row that is itself a stack of rows restarts the alternation, so its last line can share a hue with the next row. Counting lines would make a colour depend on the shape of the rows around it, the pre-order cursor's flaw; the bundled bar nests no stack inside a row. Rounding to the nearer of the two hues (the tone's rule) ties at every odd quarter, and gave a three-row bar `primary, secondary, secondary`. Which two hues is no longer fixed: see "The progression" below.
- **A cell's tone is its place in its row.** Three tones, because a row's neighbours are placed by van der Corput: its first eight cells land on tones 0, 1, ½, 1, 0, 1, ½, 0, so no two side by side share one and neighbours sit at least half the axis apart. A continuous axis gives neighbours a quarter of it (cells 1 and 2 sit at ½ and ¼), and measured under the seam floor in 42 of 46 theme × hue rows. Since 3m7 a cell is a leaf of its row, however the row nests it; the 3m7 entry below says why. The tone rounds to the nearest of three points; taking the third a placement falls in (the hue's rule) would put cells 5 and 6 of a row — inside the bundled status row — on one tone. Since e5n the three are rungs of the role's own ladder, listed so these placements walk up and down a rung at a time.
- **Edit chrome is part of its cell.** The `+`/`-` affordances were spliced as siblings, so they took two of every three positions in a row and visible neighbours were never consecutive: the calm context and cache timer landed on one tone. Each content cell and its chrome is now one horizontal unit whose children share the content's one place, and the row places content only; a content segment that is a whole row becomes the one cell of that row, so its chrome shares its colour there too. Since 3m7 a unit is the one container in a row whose children do not take places of their own.
- **The tone axis** runs from `surface` receded 0.5 toward `background` to `surface` pulled 0.35 toward the hue. Receding the deep end all the way to `background` sank the deepest cells into a terminal painted in the theme's own background. The tinted end is the largest pull tried (0.45, 0.4, 0.35) that the state floor allows: at 0.4 solarized-dark's dim foreground no longer clears 2.2 against its most-tinted cell, and its three open states collapsed onto one colour past the pole. Superseded by e5n: a cell wears its role's own shades, so there is no tone axis.
- **The open state is `accent`**, floored at 2.2 against every tint the bar can wear rather than against its own hue's, because an open trigger stands in either row at any tone. 33 of the 69 theme × hue states clear at step zero and are the pure mix (41 before the progression put `accent` tints on the bar). Since e5n the floor holds a trigger off the closed cells of its own row instead; against every colour the bar can wear, it became impossible on some themes.

Measured with the calm and hot bundled bar under every theme: neighbouring tones of one hue sit ΔE .040 apart or more on every theme but textual-ansi (median .068); the closest two themes' bars are rose-pine and rose-pine-moon at .029, unchanged. textual-ansi's hues sit at its surface's lightness, so its tones fold (.018), and there the joiner draws the powerline divider (U+E0B1, the left cell's text on its own ground) wherever two backgrounds sit within ΔE .04 (rich-js `SEAM_MIN_DELTA_E`). `audits/theme-identity.audit.ts` pins the chroma share, the theme-to-theme floor, every arrow seam at or above .04, and dividers only where the theme's own colours meet (restated under every progression and look in "The progression" below). e5n retired the chroma share and the theme-to-theme floor; the seam and divider facts stand.

**The progression: which role each row wears** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.7g6). The fixed `primary, secondary` became a setting. A progression is an ordered list of decorative roles, and row n wears step n — the same van der Corput selection as before, over the progression instead of a constant, so a two-step progression alternates and a one-step one is uniform. Three ship, named by their roles: `secondary-accent` (the default), `primary-secondary` (what shipped before), and `primary`. They are the variants Brandon compared on the 8fp decoration page, read off its rendered bytes across dracula, nord, gruvbox, tokyo-night and catppuccin-mocha: B put `secondary, accent` on the rows, D1 `primary`, and both C and D2 `primary, secondary`. C looked different on the page only because it still used the per-cell `base × amount` vocabulary that #237 replaced, which is not the seam; under the progression model C and the old default are one progression, and it ships once. (Superseded by brandon-menu-ia-q30.1s9: a variation is now `{ hues, run }`, and C ships as `alt` — the default's hues, turning every three cells along a row.) It is chosen like `style`: `globals.progression` under a session pick, with a carousel in the settings menu's `⚙ config` row.

The default puts `accent` on closed cells, which the old rule forbade at compile time. An open trigger is now told from the closed bar by contrast only, and every floor that measures against "every colour the closed bar can wear" measures against every tint ANY progression can produce — `DECOR_VOCABULARY` is the hues the progressions reach × the tones, derived from the table, never from the one selected — so a floor that holds holds whichever progression is picked. e5n replaced that with each trigger's own row and deleted `DECOR_VOCABULARY`. With the accent tints added, every state and band floor in `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts` held unchanged over every theme × look × drawn depth; 8 more theme × hue states need the search past their pure mix. Two synthetic palettes in the tests, built so the accent finds a state while the primary nested under it does not, had to be rebuilt: their accent could not clear its own tints.

`audits/theme-identity.audit.ts` now renders the bundled bar under every progression × every bundled look, and three of its facts needed restating to hold there:

- **Theme to theme.** Under the default, no look, the closest two themes are rose-pine and rose-pine-moon at .031 (.024 under `primary-secondary`). Every progression × look clears .02 except two, both under the one-hue `primary`, where two themes sharing a primary converge once a look narrows what else differs: rose-pine and rose-pine-moon at .019 under `dim`, dracula and rose-pine-moon at .015 under `bright`. Those two pairs are pinned by name; the floor is not moved. e5n retired this fact; see its entry below.
- **Chroma near black.** The `inverted` look pulls the light themes' deepest tones to near black (`#020002`), where one 8-bit channel step is most of the chroma there is: rose-pine-dawn's aim of .033 stores as .021. The chroma fact is now held against the aim as stored, not against the unstorable aim; at every other lightness the two agree within the .004 already allowed. e5n retired this fact with the chroma share.
- **Where a divider may appear.** The rule was "only in a theme whose own tones fold". Under `inverted`, atom-one-light's `warning` and its accent's middle tone both land near `#0b0b00`, so a hot threshold cell beside a closed accent cell meets it; the joiner draws the divider there as designed. The fact is now "only where the theme's own colours meet": two tones of one hue, or a tint and an alert role.

**Truecolor is the target.** `colorCompatibility` is an explicit setting and the daemon cannot detect client depth — which is why `"auto"` is deliberately unrepresentable. At 256 and ansi the vocabulary degrades toward flat, which is the floor the design already guarantees.

**At 256 colours text keeps its floor; at ansi no ratio exists** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.fe0). The contrast floors were measured on truecolor values, and the downgrade to 256 broke them twice over: it could land on ANSI 0–15, whose RGB every terminal theme redefines, and the terminal rounds text and background independently, so the two roundings can meet in the middle. rich-js now downgrades only onto the cube and grey ramp (16–255), and every place cc-candybar CHOOSES a text colour — `textOn`, the band cells, the picker's options, the `readableOn` template function — floors it on the pair the terminal will draw: `ensureContrast(fg, bg, ratio, drawnAt)`, with `drawnAt` published per render from `colorCompatibility` (`drawnDepth`, over rich-js's own `resolveColorSystem`) into the variable registry's one observable clock, so a `readableOn` inside a template variable is floored at it too. A colour that loses its floor once rounded is replaced by the nearest cube/grey entry that clears it, which rounds to itself. The SGR writer still rounds every colour on its own, as Python Rich does, and that is what keeps a powerline arrow the exact colour of the cell it continues — an arrow's foreground IS its neighbour's background, a shape rather than text, and no rule at the writer could tell the two apart. Measured on the bundled bar at 256 against its truecolor render: 1171 of 3243 text characters lost contrast and 257 landed on ANSI 0–15 before; now every character keeps the highest floor it cleared in truecolor (4.5:1, or the quiet git structure's 3:1), none lands on ANSI 0–15, and every arrow matches its cell. `test/colour-256.test.ts` holds all three. At `ansi` the terminal draws each of the sixteen colours as its own theme says, so no ratio can be computed and no ratio floor is claimed; the one provable floor there is the next paragraph's.

**The floors that are not text hold on what is drawn too** (brandon-theme-picker-bgw.ddk). fe0's measure reached text only. Three other floors were still measured on truecolor values:

- An open trigger must clear `STATE_FLOOR` against every closed tint. Measured on the drawn colours at 256, it fell short against some tint in 167 of 414 theme × look × hue cases. Since e5n the floor holds a trigger off the closed cells of its own row.
- The band ΔE floors keep a trigger, its plane and a nested band apart. At 256, eight lineages' adjacent planes rounded to a single cube entry. Since e5n a nested trigger is held off its plane by the state floor, not a ΔE floor.
- The powerline seam chose between arrow and divider on truecolor values. Two grounds that the terminal draws as one entry still got an arrow, drawn in the very colour it sat on: twice at 256, and in nine themes' closed bars at ansi.

`stateFor` and `bandFor` now take the drawn depth. A state or plane whose drawn colour breaks a floor is replaced by the nearest cube or grey entry that keeps it. That is rich-js `ensureDrawn`, the same repair `ensureContrast` makes, but with a stated floor where `ensureContrast` has a contrast ratio. A plane is held only to the floors its truecolor colours already keep, so depth 3's adjacent planes, which truecolor misses under some styles (the limit above), are not asked of 256 there either. The floors are `BAND_FLOORS` in `decor.ts`, which the tests read rather than restate.

The seam is rich-js's. The joiner now decides on the colours the render encodes (`RenderOptions.colorSystem`, measured through `Style.drawnColors`, the same colours the SGR writer encodes). It draws an arrow in its left ground as drawn, so a translucent ground is not composited a second time over the ground it enters. It draws the divider on the left cell's own ground, so a divider is that cell's text and holds that cell's text floor, never less than 3:1.

At ansi the terminal draws its own theme's sixteen colours, so every floor there is measured on the ANSI table's nominal colours, which stand in for the theme's. They are wrong in hue from theme to theme but right about which side of a ground a colour sits on, and a colour on its ground's index measures 1:1 and is always refused. `ensureContrast` and `ensureDrawn` measure ansi exactly as they measure 256, through rich-js `drawnColour`; dracula's closed bar drew three characters on index 8 before, and a nearest-other-index repair (the first version) drew 458 of 1602 random collisions below 2:1 against the nominal table.

Every floor is measured on colours as drawn, including truecolor's: a translucent theme's colours are shown composited over the terminal's black, and a ratio over raw RGBA reads colours drawn nowhere. `stateFor` searched on raw RGBA before, and cyberpunk's secondary open state cleared 2.2 there while drawing at 1.71 against its closed tints. `test/colour-256.test.ts` renders every theme's closed bar and open settings states at 256 and at ansi, and holds all of this. `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts` holds the state and band floors over every theme, look and depth, at both depths.

**`hue.step` and its stepper knob were removed with no successor** (ai7.4). Nothing real was bound to it: the maintainer's live config declared only `applyTheme`, and the `hueUp`/`hueDown` pair existed solely as an illustration in CLAUDE.md, which went with it.

**The distribution is authorable per instance, defaulting to van der Corput.** All five values ship: van der Corput, golden angle, monotonic, ends-interleaved, uniform. This is one type with five instances, not five code paths — the distribution is a value the instance carries, so adding one is data, not structure. The cost of choosing an `n`-reading value is isolation, and it is the author's to spend; see "One distribution field, at every level". Shipped (candybar-render-ai7.8) as one config key, `distribution`, on the two kinds of placer a config can spell: any container node (`{ h: [...], distribution: "monotonic" }`, `{ v: … }`, `kind: "container"`, and `kind: "group"`, where it places the body) and a `{{ menu }}`'s options dict (`(dict "distribution" "monotonic")`); the names are `van-der-corput`, `golden-angle`, `ends-interleaved`, `monotonic`, `uniform`. Each address step carries the distribution its parent places by, so a tree mixing several is several values folded by one rule.

**The closed bar wears its theme's own shades** (candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.e5n). The tones above were blends, colours no palette contains, and Brandon replaced them. On a comparison page that drew every bundled theme beside Textual's own drawing of it, in each candidate look under every variation and style, he judged the looks built from the palette "uniformly better than the current themes", short only of a sense of variety and intention the options should supply. Of the three kinds of look he kept two, shades and muted, and cut panels (cells in `surface` and `panel`): "we've gotta cut something and these don't make the cut". His decision:

> from these 2 batches, i want to keep shades+dial, and muted. these will be new themeing axes in addition to the existing axes, not a replacement for any. they will however replace the existing sort of ad-hoc theme bastardization we have today.

He leaned to the simpler of two ways to join muted to the dial — "1b. Alternatively, 'muted' is simply the lowest setting on the dial" — and asked for two more things: "design it to be flexible. the default will be 'middle' (standard role)". So the shade dial is a display setting beside `variation` and `style`: `globals.shade` under a session pick, with its `🔆` control in the `🎨 look` tab and the layout preview under its list. Its positions, lowest first, are `muted`, `deepest`, `deeper`, `middle`, `brighter` and `brightest`. Each is one row of `SHADES` in `src/themes/decor.ts`; the option domain, the loader's enum, the control and every floor derive from that table, so a new position, or a wider window, is a new row.

- **A position is three neighbouring rungs, listed low, high, middle.** Textual generates seven shades of every role, `darken-3` through the role to `lighten-3`. A position takes three neighbours, from the three darkest (`deepest`) to the three brightest (`brightest`), with `middle` centred on the role. A row's cells pick entries 0, 2, 1, 2, 0, 2, 1, 0 of three (van der Corput, read to the nearest point), so in that listing the first seven cells walk up and down the window a rung at a time — low, middle, high, middle, low, middle, high — instead of jumping between its ends, and turning the dial one position moves every cell exactly one rung. It is also the order the comparison page drew the windows in, so the bars ship as Brandon saw them. Since tx7 (candybar-joiners-e5r4.tx7) van der Corput is read by halves, not to the nearest point, because the nearest point put places 2ᵏ − 1 and 2ᵏ (7 and 8 first) on one tone: every odd place takes the middle rung and the even places alternate the ends, so the walk low, middle, high, middle repeats however long the row. The rule belongs to the distribution: golden angle reads by equal bins, which keep its neighbours apart over three tones, and the even spreads read by equal bins too, so they ramp evenly in order.
- **`muted` is one colour per row, written in the role's own text colour.** Every cell of a muted row wears `<role>-muted`, as Textual's labels and flat buttons do; with one colour across the row, the joiner parts the cells with its divider. The text is Textual's own `text-<role>`, its hue kept and its lightness slid only as far as the 4.5:1 floor needs. As the themes ship, all 69 theme × role pairs already clear that floor, the lowest at 4.50, so the text is drawn exactly as Textual names it; a style can pull it under, as far as 2.60 under `inverted`, and then the slide lifts it (both measured over every bundled theme and style). Every other position writes in the theme pole that reads on the cell, as before. A cell takes its decoration's text wherever it wears its tint, so a threshold resting on its calm `(tint)` arm reads like its neighbours. Text an author coloured, such as the git accents and the cache timer, is untouched: Brandon asked that this change remove none. Since tx7 a muted row alternates `<role>-muted` with the role itself, both under the role's text, because one colour across a row puts every pair of neighbours on one shade.
- **An open trigger is held off its own row.** The state floor, 2.2:1, held an open trigger off every colour any variation could put on the bar. With the roles' own shades on the bar, that cannot hold. At the default position no colour stands 2.2:1 off every shade of textual-dark's or textual-light's three roles, among them a navy (`#004578`) and an orange (`#FEA62B`), nor off rose-pine's at `deepest`; even against the default bar's own two rows, textual-dark leaves none. A trigger stands among the closed cells of its own row, so it is held off exactly the colours those cells wear (`rowGround`), read by the same rule that colours them. When this change first held a trigger off every shade the dial gives its row's roles, the search reported a shortfall on 65 rows that some colour clears, among them the bundled bar's first row on rose-pine at `deepest` under `alt` (counted over every theme, style, shade, variation, row start and row length up to twelve cells). Per row the floor fails only under `alt`, on a row turning between two roles that straddle the middle grey, where no colour at all clears: 16 of the 828 theme × style × shade combinations, and under the themes as they ship rose-pine at `deepest` and textual-dark at `deeper`, `middle` and `brighter`. (Counted over every bundled theme, style and shade.) On the bundled bar it is the second row that falls short on rose-pine at `deepest` and on textual-dark at `deeper`, and both rows on textual-dark at `middle` and `brighter`.
- **A shortfall is reported, not thrown.** On a row no colour clears, the trigger takes the candidate standing furthest off its nearest cell as drawn. At 256 colours or ansi, whichever colour the search settled on — one that clears, or that furthest one — is replaced by the nearest colour the depth draws that clears every cell as drawn, where one exists, and kept where none does; the shortfall is measured on the pair as drawn. (Before this, on a row nothing cleared, the report gave truecolor's number while naming the drawn depth: textual-dark under `alt` at 256 colours said 1.70:1 where the drawn pair stands at 1.82:1.) In truecolor no colour clears textual-dark's first row, the one the settings door leads, under `alt` at the default `middle` shade: the open door is drawn white (`#ffffff`), 1.96:1 off its nearest cell. At 256 colours it now clears, drawn black; at ansi nothing the depth draws clears, and the door is drawn light grey (`#c0c0c0`), the candidate standing furthest off its cells as ansi draws them, reported at 1.69:1 (the white truecolor picks stands 1.07:1 there). The render reports each distinct shortfall once, as a `band-floor` render warning; at colour depth `none`, which draws no colour, it reports none. `cc-candybar check` lists it as a warning, tagged with the presets that reach it as a failure is, and passes, while a `globals.palette` rule that names no theme still fails check; both used to reach check as one "theme rule" failure, so a shortfall failed check under the wrong name and the two could overwrite each other. The daemon shows a shortfall on the amber diagnostic strip: `theme "<name>": no open-trigger colour stands 2.2:1 off every cell beside it — the nearest stands N:1 as drawn at depth X; another shade or variation clears it`. The search used to throw, but the settings door is a trigger too, and a throw there would take away the control that turns the setting back.
- **The search slides the hue along its own lightness.** The state is the hue exactly as the palette names it when that already clears. Otherwise it is the hue with its angle and chroma kept, moved toward white and toward black in twelfths, one step each way in turn, and the first candidate that clears wins. The search it replaced blended toward the theme's poles, which greyed the hue, and a slide past a pole, folded cell by cell with `ensureContrast`, undid itself on cells straddling the middle grey: on atom-one-dark's deepest `secondary` row the background pole got pulled toward white. `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts` holds the new search to the independent luminance test: it falls short exactly where no colour clears. Where no twelfth clears, the search offers every colour the terminal draws along both rays, nearest first, and takes the first that clears; where none at all does, whichever stands furthest off its nearest cell as drawn: two cells adjacent in luminance leave a window of colours standing 2.2:1 off both exactly when they stand 2.2² apart, since a contrast ratio multiplies, and the colour at their balance stands the root of their ratio off each. Rows an authored `distribution` deals on textual-dark leave such windows as narrow as .002 of luminance between its navy and its orange, which the twelfths stepped over and reported short; where nothing clears, the drawable colour nearest the balance of the widest gap is as far as any stands, up to .19 further than the furthest twelfth (textual-dark under `bright`). The drawable colours are enumerated, not sampled (`RAY_HALVINGS`): rich-js clamps the ray into gamut before rounding, so each channel is continuous along it and two colours one channel step apart have no other between them, and a stretch whose ends are further apart is halved until every pair is. Two searches of the ray preceded it (brandon-menus-sjhy.4fi). A balance found by halving: once a trigger is also told from the items of its band (`apart`), its own hue's shades and so on its ray, the balance could land on an item while colours either side of it cleared both floors — 34 shipped bands under the `none` style alone wore an avoidable seam. Then a bisection for each constraint's boundary, which assumed the predicates monotone along the ray: a single blue step took a drawn colour back under an item's seam (catppuccin-mocha under `muted`, depth 4 — told, not told, told across three neighbouring colours), the bisection met whichever crossing it landed on, and the one drawable colour between was never offered. A window the analytic bound admits can hold no drawable colour of the hue — three rows (dracula under `muted`, textual-dark under `bright`) leave windows under .002 of luminance wide that both rays step over — so `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts` holds the search to its own walk of the drawable colours, not to the bound. The furthest is judged as drawn because a balance sits between two cells and, at 256 colours or ansi, rounds onto one where an end of the axis does not: judged in truecolor, 150 searches across the registry stood nearer their cells as drawn than the twelfth they replaced (candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.6h1).
- **A plane recedes toward whichever pole leaves it a band.** The plane is still the state receded toward `background` by its depth's recession, unless that plane breaks a band floor: ΔE .10 off its own trigger, or .035 off the plane it hangs in. Then it recedes toward `foreground`, then toward either as far as the cap, then the twelfth of the way to either pole nearest the recession, taking the first that holds both floors, else the background one. A state the search carried all the way to the background, to stand off a vivid row on a light theme (solarized-light, catppuccin-latte), had no room to recede into it and collapsed onto its own plane. Nor is it only a light-theme case: 781 of the 5,106 bands the bundled themes can open at depths 0–2 take another plane, and every theme but textual-ansi has some (measured with `bandFor` over every theme and every set of shades a row's cells can wear, unstyled).
- **A band item is drawn, never thrown.** A band's items are its hue at every tone of the dial's window, as the palette names them (brandon-menus-sjhy.4fi; before it, two colours blended between its plane and its trigger), the state searched to stand ΔE .04 off every one and, at 256 colours and ansi, each repaired to the nearest drawn colour standing ΔE .04 off the trigger, a trigger open in the band and the items before it, and not the plane (brandon-menus-sjhy.b9w). Where the depth draws no such colour, the item is drawn at its place and the pair it stands too near is reported — in truecolor, under every bundled theme and style, only textual-dark under `dim` at depth 0. Only a band whose plane sits on its trigger has no room at all, and a style that washes the palette out leaves one: in a sweep of extreme lightness and chroma styles over every theme, 51 of 1,449 theme × style pairs, mostly in the menu's tabs and pickers. There the item is drawn at its place, and the band reports its plane as a `band-floor` warning beside the state's: `theme "<name>": an open band's plane stands ΔE N off the trigger that opened it as drawn at depth X, under the 0.1 that tells them apart; another style clears it`. Every one of those used to throw and take the settings controls with it, the reason the state search does not throw either. No bundled theme reports it under any bundled style (0 of 40,848 bands at depths 0–3). The plane itself always has a colour: at 256 colours and ansi some entry keeps all of its floors wherever its neighbours are drawn (at least 40 of 240, and 5 of 16), so its repair cannot fail, and its throw stays as the guard on that, pinned by `audits/decor-registry.audit.ts`.
- **A nested trigger is held off the plane it sits on.** A trigger inside a band, such as a tab or a picker's control, stands on its parent band's plane rather than on the bar, so the state floor holds it 2.2:1 off that plane, and at 256 colours and ansi the drawn plane's repair keeps that contrast. Held off nothing, nested states sat ΔE .03–.07 from their parent planes on several themes; held off the bar's row as well, a nested state was driven to the same end of the lightness axis as its parent on a vivid row, and the two collapsed into one. The contrast floor replaces `BAND_FLOORS.nestedTriggerPlane`, the ΔE .08 that held a nested trigger off its enclosing plane, which is deleted.
- **The door's ✖ is a red its theme names, chosen to read on the door** (candybar-joiners-e5r4.i30). The open door's ✖ is never slid toward a contrast floor, so that it reads as red on every theme (#294): a red slid until it reads stops reading as red. It is `keptOn (roleTextOn "error" (bgOf)) (bgOf)`. `roleTextOn` selects among the reds the theme names for its error role, each measured on the door as truecolor draws it: `text-error` where it clears 4.5:1; otherwise the first that clears of the base and its ladder's rungs, walking out from the base toward the pole the door stands farther from; and where none clears, whichever of them, `text-error` and the base included, stands farthest off. Since dg6 the door opens on its menu hue's muted form, where the base red stood at 1.49:1 on `default`; `text-error` stands at 3.84:1 there. On every bundled theme but textual-ansi, some ground a variation and dial position open the door on leaves no named red reaching 4.5:1, and there the ✖ reads under the floor rather than slid, at worst 2.08:1 (rose-pine on `secondary-lighten-2`, a trial variation's ground); `test/door-close-contrast.test.ts` lists each such ground. At 256 colours and ansi `keptOn` returns the nearest colour that depth draws whose contrast with the drawn door is at least what the red has in truecolor, clamped to between 3:1 and 4.5:1, so rounding never costs it. (Measured over every bundled theme, dial position and variation, trial tunings included, unstyled.)
- **Checked against what Brandon saw.** Every file the comparison page rendered for the six positions — 288 of them, one per position, variation, style and calm or hot state, each holding every bundled theme's bar — was rendered again through this change's code, and 250 were byte-identical. The other 38 are all `muted` under a style other than `none`, and they differed only in text the page drew under 4.5:1 and the code lifts to the floor; no background and no character differed. Main's joiner change (#324) has since changed how the powerline joiners are drawn, so no file is byte-identical now, but every other character, and the background it sits on, still matches in all 288. The page is never committed; the epic `candybar-theme-colours-6fq5` says where it lives. Those are closed bars. Brandon has not yet seen the open menus: on a vivid row an open trigger is the accent slid deep, since it must stand 2.2:1 off its row, and its bands follow it, so they look different from before.
- **What the tests hold.** `audits/theme-identity.audit.ts`'s first fact is now the ticket's done-when: every background the bundled closed bar draws, calm and hot, under every variation and every position of the dial, unstyled, on every bundled theme, is within ΔE .02 of a colour in its theme's palette. In practice each is one exactly: all 87,216 backgrounds of those 1104 bars are palette colours byte for byte (counted by rendering them as the test does). The theme-to-theme fact, which held two themes' bars ΔE .02 apart cell by cell, is retired. It guarded against synthesis eroding one theme into its neighbour; with no synthesis a bar is its theme's own roles, and two themes that share roles share a bar. Solarized dark and light name all seven shades of every role the bar wears alike, so their bars wear identical backgrounds at every position but `muted`, whose shades blend into each theme's own background.
- **Accepted.** textual-dark's and textual-light's `accent` is their `warning`, `#FEA62B`, so under the `accent` and `alt` variations their calm accent cells wear warning orange. Brandon accepted that, to be configured away with per-theme defaults (candybar-theme-colours-6fq5.ix8).
- **Deleted:** the blended tones and their constants, the mix the state search started from, the search's throw, and the nested trigger's ΔE floor. "What was deleted" lists the symbols.

**An open menu wears named shades, and nothing is measured between cells** (candybar-joiners-e5r4.dg6). Brandon ruled that the contrast floors around open menus were never his ("I didn't add or ask for those contrast rules"), and rich-js's joiner, not the colour policy, owns every seam; the reasoning is `design-docs/PROPOSAL-joiners.md`. The pressed state is one more column of the shade dial: the menu's hue at its muted form, as Textual's flat buttons wear it, or at `lighten-2` on the `muted` position, a rung that row never deals. The trigger wears it, and so do the ✕ and blank leads of its band's rows. The plane is `surface` at every depth, and the items are unchanged. A band's colours are lookups of palette, lineage, depth and dial, unrounded, and the SGR writer rounds each once. This supersedes the e5n bullets on holding an open trigger off its row, reporting a shortfall, the state search, the plane's recession, the band item repair and the nested trigger's floor, and the ddk entry's first two bullets with its drawn repair of states and planes. Where a theme names two neighbours alike (textual-ansi's three muted forms are one blue, and `default`'s `accent-muted` is its `primary-muted`), the joiner shows the seam.

**A row's leaves take its places, however the row nests them** (candybar-joiners-e5r4.3m7). A container inside a row was one address, so everything in it wore one shade: Brandon's status row groups context, the auto-compact window and a plugin's cell in one `{ h }`, and the three drew one orange; the settings door is built as one node with its frame-rate cell, and the two drew one blue. Cells that sit side by side are what must differ, and grouping is layout, so the compile now places every leaf of a row at its place on the row's drawn line, through any nested `{ h }`; a `{ v }` in a row draws its first child on the row's line and each other on a line of its own below, each counted from where the `{ v }` sits, and the row's count is the farthest place any of its lines reaches, so a distribution that reads the count places every leaf inside it. A keystone reads the ends of the line its cell is drawn on, not the count: on a row whose lower line reaches further than its own, the row line's last leaf still ends it. Places come from the authored tree alone, so hiding a cell still recolours none, and authoring a cell inside a group moves the shades after it, as authoring one in the row does. The row's distribution places every leaf under it, so the loader refuses a `distribution` written anywhere inside a row. Edit chrome is the one exception: its unit holds the content, the names-view label that stands in for it, and the `+` insert triggers beside it, each a cell of its own; given places, they would push every content cell along and entering edit mode would recolour the bar, so a unit's children share the content's one place. The unit is a mark on a horizontal container, not a node kind of its own, because it renders exactly as one and differs only in where its children are placed; no config can write it. This supersedes the e5n bullet's "anything nested inside a cell wears the cell's hue and tone".

The design stands on those measurements, not on the line count.

And the replacement direction it stated — authoring `bg: '{{ shiftHue (color "surface") 14 }}'` per segment — was exactly the hand-curation this design rules out. Both sentences went with the mechanism.
